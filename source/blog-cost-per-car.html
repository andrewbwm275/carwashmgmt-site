What Is Cost Per Car (CPC) and Why It Matters for Car Wash Profitability

Cost Per Car is the single most important metric in car wash operations — and most owners have never calculated it. Here's what it is, how to calculate it, and how to use it to run a more profitable wash.

What Is Cost Per Car (CPC)?

Cost Per Car is the total variable cost to wash one vehicle. It includes every consumable and service that goes into each car: chemicals, water, electricity, labor (per car), and maintenance amortized over volume.

CPC Formula:

CPC = (Total Variable Monthly Costs) ÷ (Total Cars Washed)

For most tunnel operations, a healthy CPC falls between $2.50–$4.50. If yours is above $5.00, you have identifiable inefficiencies worth addressing immediately.

What Goes Into CPC?

  • Chemistry
  • $0.40–$0.90/car
  • Presoak, triple foam, protectants, wheel cleaner
  • Water
  • $0.10–$0.40/car
  • Depends heavily on reclaim system performance
  • Electricity
  • $0.30–$0.70/car
  • Motors, lighting, HVAC, control systems
  • Labor (variable)
  • $0.80–$1.50/car
  • Prorated attendant time per vehicle throughput
  • Maintenance
  • $0.15–$0.40/car
  • PM costs and repair reserves amortized per car

How CPC Connects to Profit Margin

Your gross margin per car is simply your average ticket price minus your CPC. If your average ticket is $14 and your CPC is $5.20, you're running a 63% gross margin — which sounds good until you compare it to operators at 72–78% who've done the work to optimize costs.

At 300 cars per day, closing that margin gap from 63% to 72% is worth $9,855/month in additional gross profit — on the same car count, same pricing.

How to Calculate Your CPC Right Now

  • Step 1
  • Pull last month's invoices for chemicals, water, electricity, and maintenance.
  • Step 2
  • Add labor costs for wash-related staff (not management or admin).
  • Step 3
  • Divide total by your monthly car count from the POS or counter.
  • Step 4
  • Compare each category against benchmarks. The highest variance is your biggest opportunity.

What Drives CPC Above Benchmark?

  • Chemistry dilution ratios not calibrated to water profile
  • Failing reclaim system increasing effective water cost
  • Deferred maintenance creating higher repair costs per car
  • Over-staffing relative to throughput volume
  • Energy waste from aging motors and inefficient dryers

The Bottom Line

CPC isn't a finance term — it's your operational scorecard. Operators who track it monthly find and fix problems faster, negotiate better supplier terms, and consistently run higher margins than those who don't.
